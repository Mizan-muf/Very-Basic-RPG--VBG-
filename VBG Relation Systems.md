# VBG: Relationship & Bond Systems

Relationship systems give mechanical weight to the social fabric of the story — the people characters care about, owe, trust, and fear. Five options are presented below, ranging from minimal to structured. A recommendation for VBG follows.

---

## System 1: Bond Tokens (Minimal)

**How it works:**
Characters form **Bonds** through shared significant moments. When two characters (PC or NPC) experience something meaningful together — saved each other, shared a secret, survived a crisis, swore an oath — they gain a **Bond Token** with each other.

**Using a Bond Token:**
- Once per scene, spend a Bond Token to **ignore the roll entirely** on any action taken specifically to protect, help, or save the bonded character.
- Narrative trump card: love, loyalty, or desperation drives you past your limits.
- Bond Tokens refresh at the start of each new story arc.

**Breaking a Bond:**
If a character betrays, abandons, or causes direct harm to a bonded character, the Bond breaks. It cannot be reformed until meaningful restitution is made and accepted — both in narrative and agreed upon by both players.

**Complexity:** Very Low.
**Best for:** One-shots, new players, stories emphasizing found family and sacrifice.

## System 2: Trust Track (Structured Relationship Growth)

**How it works:**
Each significant relationship has a **Trust Track** with five levels:

`0 — Stranger | 1 — Acquaintance | 2 — Ally | 3 — Trusted | 4 — Bonded`

**Gaining Trust:** After a scene where you acted in the relationship's genuine interest (at cost to yourself), the GM may award +1 Trust.

**Losing Trust:** Betrayals, broken promises, cowardice when it mattered, or deliberate harm costs −1 or −2 Trust depending on severity.

**Mechanical Effects by Trust Level:**

| Level | Effect |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | No mechanical benefit |
| 2 (Ally) | During a Rest Scene shared with this character, recover +1 extra Strain |
| 3 (Trusted) | GM lowers DR by 5 for any action taken directly to protect or rescue this character |
| 4 (Bonded) | Once per story arc — if you would enter Last Stand, you may instead treat it as if you rolled a Clean Success automatically. The bond pulls you back. |

**Complexity:** Low-Medium. One number per tracked relationship.
**Best for:** Campaign play, evolving relationships, stories where trust is a central theme.

## System 3: Relationship Web (Narrative Map)

**How it works:**
No mechanical stats. Each player maintains a **Relationship Web** — a diagram or list showing their active connections and a one-word descriptor for each.

**Format:**
```
[Your Character] ←→ [Name] — Respect
[Your Character] ←→ [Name] — Debt
[Your Character] ←→ [Name] — Rivalry
[Your Character] ←→ [Name] — Love (strained)
```

**Mechanical Hook:**
The GM uses the Web to:
- Design scenes that pressure specific relationships
- Target NPCs the character cares about during threats
- Award 1–2 PP for meaningful relationship milestones in an arc

**Complexity:** Near zero mechanically. Moderate narrative investment.
**Best for:** Writers, async play, roleplay-heavy groups. Ideal when the story IS the relationships.

## System 4: Reputation Tiers (Faction & Group Standing)

**How it works:**
Tracks standing with **organizations, factions, or groups** rather than individual characters.

**Five Tiers:** `Unknown → Noticed → Known → Respected → Legendary`

**Gaining Reputation:** Completing faction-relevant missions, upholding faction values, making sacrifices on the faction's behalf.

**Losing Reputation:** Embarrassing the faction publicly, working against their interests, being caught in a lie with them.

**Mechanical Benefits by Tier:**

| Tier | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Noticed | Members of this faction will speak to you and take meetings |
| Known | Access to faction resources: gear, safe houses, basic information |
| Respected | GM lowers DR by 5 for any action taken in faction territory or with faction support; members assist narratively |
| Legendary | Once per arc, call in a faction-wide favor: reinforcements, political influence, a rare item, or access to a guarded location |

**Complexity:** Low. One tier per faction.
**Best for:** Political campaigns, guild-based stories, war arcs, city-based intrigue.

## System 5: Heart System (Deep Investment — Ironsworn-Inspired)

**How it works:**
Each significant NPC or PC relationship is given a **Heart** — a descriptor and a bond strength from 1 to 3.

| Strength | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 Heart | Acquaintance / Situational Ally — you know each other and would help if it wasn't costly |
| 2 Hearts | True Ally / Close Friend — you would take real risk for this person |
| 3 Hearts | Sworn Bond / Family / Love — you would sacrifice yourself without hesitation |

**Forming Hearts:** Declared by the player after a meaningful scene. Costs nothing but must be narratively earned and agreed upon (for PC–PC bonds, both players must consent).

**Mechanical Weight:**

| Event | Consequence |
|---|---|
| A 3-Heart NPC is killed or permanently lost | Character gains **Grief Condition** (−1d10 from their pool) until resolved through narrative action |
| A 2-Heart character saves your life | Gain the **Indebted** condition — you must act in their direct interest in a future scene before it clears |
| Two PCs both hold 3 Hearts with each other | Once per story arc: if either enters Last Stand while the other is present and uses their action to help, the Last Stand is an automatic Clean Success |

**Complexity:** Medium. Requires active tracking of heart levels and triggered conditions.
**Best for:** Character-driven drama, long campaigns, async games with deep emotional investment.

## Recommendation for VBG

**Three-layer approach — scaled to importance:**

### Layer 1: Relationship Web (All Relationships)
Every character maintains a Web. This costs nothing, requires no tracking, fits naturally into async post writing, and gives the GM fuel for scenes. Default for every connection.

### Layer 2: Trust Track (Key Relationships Only)
For the 2–3 relationships that matter most to a character — a mentor, a rival, a love interest, a sworn enemy — apply a Trust Track on top of the Web. This gives those specific connections mechanical stakes without burdening every NPC with numbers.

### Layer 3: Reputation Tiers (Factions, as needed)
If the campaign involves guilds, noble houses, criminal organizations, or religious orders, layer Reputation Tiers onto those groups. One tier number per faction, referenced at the GM level.

**Result:**
- Most relationships: narrative only (the Web).
- Relationships that matter most: one number (Trust Track).
- Faction standing: one tier per group (Reputation).

This keeps VBG's "Very Basic" character while giving meaningful relationship mechanics where the story needs them.

## Quick Reference Table

| System | Tracks | Complexity | Ideal Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bond Tokens | Shared moments → token | Very Low | One-shots, new players |
| Trust Track | 0–4 per relationship | Low-Medium | Campaign arcs, evolving bonds |
| Relationship Web | Narrative map | Near Zero | Async/roleplay-heavy games |
| Reputation Tiers | Unknown → Legendary per faction | Low | Faction/political campaigns |
